## 1. Iran strikes US bases in Bahrain (conflict)

Iran's Revolutionary Guards said they hit two US military positions in
Bahrain, at al-Riffa and Awali. Bahrain said the attack hit a civilian
industrial facility and started a fire, with no injuries, and that its air
defences intercepted other attacks. Iran also said it downed a US drone near
Qeshm Island; the US denied Iran's claim that it hit an F-16.

## 2. Mexico captures "El Chapo Isidro" (justice)

Mexican special forces captured Fausto Isidro Meza Flores, known as Chapo
Isidro, in a Friday-night raid in Guasave, Sinaloa. He was on the FBI's ten
most-wanted list. The arrest set off gunfights, fires and road blockades;
at least 11 people died (four soldiers, seven attackers) and eight were
detained.

## 3. COP31 climate talks run past deadline (climate)

The UN climate summit in Antalya, Turkey, ran past its Friday deadline and
through Saturday into the early hours of Sunday without a deal. Late on
Saturday the summit president announced a new package he hoped would win
consensus, with an approval session set for Sunday morning. Fossil fuels and
climate finance were the sticking points.

## 5. Wolbachia mosquitoes cut dengue 69% in Brazil trial (health)

A four-year cluster-randomised trial in Belo Horizonte, Brazil, found about
69% fewer dengue and chikungunya infections among schoolchildren in
neighbourhoods where Wolbachia-carrying mosquitoes were released, compared
with similar neighbourhoods. The trial enrolled 5,757 children aged 6 to 11
and tested blood for infection rather than counting diagnosed cases. The
results were presented at a tropical-medicine meeting on Saturday; they are
preliminary and not yet peer-reviewed.

## 6. 6.7 earthquake shakes central Peru (disaster)

A magnitude 6.7 earthquake struck central Peru at 19:09 local time on
Friday (00:09 GMT Saturday), centred near Junin in the Andes at a depth of
about 129 km, and was felt in Lima, where residents fled into the streets.
The later official balance was 1 dead and 16 injured, with homes damaged
and power cut for thousands. Note: the US Geological Survey and Peru's
Geophysical Institute put the magnitude at 6.7; Germany's GFZ measured 6.8.
The image uses 6.7.
